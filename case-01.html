Introducing Agentic AI

Designing the path from discovery to trust and activation.

PRODUCT DESIGNER·B2B SAAS · PROPERTY MANAGEMENT

Some product details and visuals have been simplified or omitted to protect confidential work.

FROM SOFTWARE TO DELEGATION

This project sat at a shift from assistive AI to a more agentic model, one where the system doesn't just help, it acts.

The relationship between user and product was changing.

"What am I delegating?"

"Where do I remain in control?"

Answering them was the real onboarding problem.

THE CHALLENGE

Buildium was exploring how to introduce agentic AI capabilities into an established B2B property-management platform.

Unlike traditional software features, autonomous systems require a new mental model: users need to understand not only what the system can do, but what happens when they delegate work to it.

The challenge was to make a complex new interaction model understandable without asking users to trust autonomy blindly.

CENTRAL PRODUCT QUESTION

"How might we help property managers understand and experience Agentic AI before asking them to trust it with their business?"

WHAT WE LEARNED

"Trust has to be earned before adoption happens."

01 · UNDERSTANDING

Users needed to understand cause and effect before feeling comfortable using autonomous AI.

02 · CONSEQUENCES

Users wanted to understand before delegating: what the system would do, and what would happen as a result.

03 · CONTROL

Visible oversight and reversibility mattered. Users needed to feel they'd remain in control.

04 · LEARNING BY EXPERIENCE

Users understood unfamiliar AI behavior better when they could learn through experience than through explanation alone.

KEY PRODUCT DECISIONS · 03
01 · Trust before spectacle
ASSUMPTION

We explored ways to demonstrate the value of the AI experience immediately.

RESEARCH

Testing showed that simulated activity could create the opposite effect: users were uncomfortable seeing activity they had not initiated or configured.

DECISION

Prioritize a transparent first state rather than creating the impression that autonomous work was already happening.

LEARNING

Before demonstrating autonomy, users first needed to understand what the AI was doing, why it was happening, and what remained under their control.

Showing pre-populated activity was intended to demonstrate the value of AI immediately. Instead, it raised a more fundamental question for users: was the system already taking action without their input or approval?

01 / INITIAL IDEA

SHOW ACTIVITY

Demonstrate value immediately by showing activity in the first Command Center experience.

02 / USER REACTION

"DID I AUTHORIZE THIS?"

Activity without context created uncertainty about whether AI was already acting.

03 / DECISION

START TRANSPARENT

Do not imply AI is already acting before the user has initiated or configured it.

Before demonstrating autonomy, users first needed to understand what the AI was doing, why it was happening, and what remained under their control.

02 · Reduce the commitment step
BEFORE

An early activation concept required users to move through multiple confirmation steps.

ITERATION

The experience was simplified into a more focused confirmation moment.

DESIGN EVOLUTION

Shown as design evolution rather than a research-driven outcome.

MULTI-STEP → SIMPLIFIED CONFIRMATION
03 · Show, don't just explain
PROBLEM

Agentic AI introduced unfamiliar behaviors that were difficult to communicate through explanatory content alone.

RESEARCH & DIRECTION

Interactive demonstration helped participants understand how the system behaved and where human oversight remained. It prioritized experiential learning over passive explanation.

HUMAN OVERSIGHT

The agent could progress independently, while returning control when a human decision was required.

DESIGN CONCEPT · NOT CONFIRMED AS SHIPPED
SIMPLIFIED EXPERIENCE MODEL

The onboarding experience was designed as a progression from understanding to delegation, gradually increasing the user's commitment.

DISCOVER→UNDERSTAND→CONFIGURE→ACTIVATE→OVERSEE

Simplified journey for portfolio presentation.

THE PROBLEM EVOLVED

As the work progressed, onboarding was not a one-time introduction. We began exploring how it could reduce setup effort further and help users reach relevant AI capabilities with less manual configuration.

The work raised a broader design question: "How could the product continue building confidence as users delegated more responsibility to AI?"

EXPLORATION · WORK IN PROGRESS
OUTCOME
VALIDATED LEARNING

Research consistently surfaced trust, comprehension and control as important adoption factors.

ASSUMPTION CHALLENGED

Testing changed an early assumption about how autonomous activity should be presented during onboarding.

DESIGN DIRECTION

The findings informed how the team approached education, activation and progressive exposure to AI capabilities.

ONGOING EXPLORATION

The work also revealed opportunities to reduce onboarding effort further.

REFLECTION

ACTIVATION WAS THE METRIC.
TRUST WAS THE BARRIER.

This project changed how I think about AI onboarding. Adoption of autonomous systems cannot be treated purely as an activation funnel.

Before users delegate meaningful work, they need to understand what the system will do, see where they remain in control, and develop enough confidence to delegate the next step.

Designing AI adoption also means designing the conditions under which trust can be earned.
