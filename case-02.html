Expanding eSignatures beyond the lease

Buildium's eSignature experience was originally focused on lease workflows. Over several releases, we expanded it to support more documents, signer types and day-to-day property management workflows.

Some product details and visuals have been simplified or omitted to protect confidential work.

WHY ESIGNATURES MATTERED

Property managers regularly need signatures on documents beyond leases: addenda, management agreements, vendor contracts and other operational paperwork. When Buildium didn't support a workflow, customers moved the document to an external signing tool and brought it back in manually.

WHAT WE LEARNED
01 · SIGNING NEEDS WENT BEYOND THE LEASE

All 9 customers in the study wanted eSignature support for documents outside the lease.

Design implication: support more document types and signer relationships.

02 · DOCUMENTS WERE EXPECTED IN CONTEXT

Customers expected signed documents where they already managed the tenant, vendor or owner relationship.

Design implication: explore contextual access, not only a central eSignature hub.

EXPANDING THE SIGNING FLOW

How should the flow change when the signer is no longer a known lease participant? The existing flow could rely on known lease participants. Supporting non-lease documents meant the sender first had to identify the type of signer, then find the right person.

AFTER LAUNCH

Customer feedback highlighted two remaining gaps.

DOCUMENT FINDABILITY

Customers still expected signed documents in the context of the relevant tenant, owner or vendor relationship.

WORKFLOWS STILL OUTSIDE BUILDIUM

Templates and merge fields continued to appear in feedback, so some workflows still required external signing tools.

WHAT I LEARNED

This project taught me to look closely at the assumptions behind an existing workflow before extending it. Some patterns scaled well. Others needed to change as new documents, signers and access needs were introduced.

Research helped shape each release, while customer feedback after launch showed where the experience still needed work.
