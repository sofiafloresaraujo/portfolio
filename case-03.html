Automating the Coordination, Not the Decision

Property managers were coordinating rental-owner renewal approvals through emails, calls and manual follow-up. I explored how AI could move the process forward without taking the final decision away from the owner.

THE PROBLEM

Renewal approvals often happened outside Buildium. Property managers followed up with rental owners by email or phone, tracked responses manually and sometimes created their own rules to keep renewals moving.

A MULTI-ACTOR WORKFLOW

This was not a single-user workflow. The property manager, rental owner, tenant, AI and the platform all had different roles.

PMC

Prepares offers, configures approval rules, reviews status, follows up.

AI

Recommends, summarizes, drafts, tracks, reminds, moves the workflow forward.

RENTAL OWNER

Reviews, adjusts, approves, declines, authorizes.

TENANT

Receives the approved renewal offer.

WHAT AI HANDLES, WHAT THE USER CONTROLS

DESIGN REVIEW

Design review challenged some of the assumptions behind the first settings model.

01

WHERE SHOULD THESE SETTINGS LIVE?

Lumina AI settings vs standard product settings

The initial direction grouped the approval configuration with other AI controls. Reviewers questioned whether some of that configuration was ordinary workflow setup rather than AI behavior.

02

HOW SIMPLE COULD THE APPROVAL MODEL BE?

The first settings model supported several approval choices and exceptions. Review feedback questioned whether the experience could be reduced to clearer choices.

03

WHAT PART OF THIS IS ACTUALLY AI?

"What part of this project is AI?"

This was a practical scope question. It exposed the need to distinguish AI-specific behavior from ordinary renewal configuration.

MAKING APPROVAL VISIBLE

Once owner approval became part of the renewal workflow, the property manager needed to understand more than whether an offer existed. They needed to know how urgent the renewal was, whether approval was required, who needed to respond and what action was available next.

The goal was to move coordination that previously lived across inboxes and memory into visible product state.

THE MOMENT AI HAS TO STOP

Generating a renewal offer was not the end of the workflow. When owner approval was required, the system had to stop progressing the renewal and make that dependency explicit.

The first version also kept the owner's interaction in email rather than introducing a separate portal flow.

AI has generated the offer, but the workflow cannot continue until the owner responds.

OWNER ACCEPTED

The owner's decision changes the workflow state and allows the process to continue.

This was the boundary the design was built around. AI could prepare the offer, explain it, track the response and keep the process moving. But when the renewal affected the owner's property and revenue, approval remained a separate human decision rather than another automated step.

WHAT I LEARNED

This project made the boundary between automation and authority very concrete. AI could remove coordination work around the decision without becoming the decision-maker.

One thing I would change is the research mix. We tested the workflow with property managers, but not directly with rental owners. For a product built around owner approval, I would include that decision-maker in the next research round.
